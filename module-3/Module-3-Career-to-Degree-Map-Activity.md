# Career-to-Degree Map Activity

This activity bridges your career interests and your academic options. You will research two specific career paths, identify what they require, and map those requirements back onto potential degree combinations.

## Step 2 — Research Each Career Path

For each career, use O*NET, the BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook, LinkedIn, and any other credible source to gather the following:

### Career 1

**Career Title:**
[Airline Information Systems/Database Specialist]

**Brief Description** *(what does this person do day to day?):*
[They keep the airline's computer systems and databases running smoothly and safely every day. They make sure flight schedules, crew assignments, and passenger bookings stay organized without crashing. They also fix system bugs, back up important data, and stop hackers from stealing information.]

**Typical Education Required:**
[A 4-year college degree (Bachelor's) in Information Science, Computer Science, or Cybersecurity.]

**Key Skills Required** *(list at least 6):*
1.Working with Databases (SQL)
2.Fixing Computer and Software Problems
3.Keeping Systems Safe from Hackers
4.Writing Simple Code (Python)
5.Spotting and Fixing Tech Errors Quickly
6.Managing Online Cloud Storage

**Key Knowledge Areas Required:**
[Computer databases, cybersecurity basics, network safety rules, cloud storage, and airline flight operations.]

**Median Annual Salary:**
[About $104,000 a year.]

**Job Growth Outlook** *(growing, stable, declining?):*
[Growing (tech jobs in databases and cybersecurity are in high demand across large companies and airlines).]

**Typical Career Path** *(how do people enter and advance in this field?):*
[Get a degree in Information Science → Start in an entry-level IT or data job at an airline → Move up to Database Administrator or Security Analyst → Advance to IT Team Lead or Manager.]

**3 specific courses or experiences that would build skills for this career:**
1.A college course in Database Management (learning SQL)
2.A college course in Cybersecurity & Network Safety
3.A summer IT internship with a major airline (like Delta or United)

### Career 2

**Career Title:**
[Professional Track and Field Athlete (Sprinter)]

**Brief Description** *(what does this person do day to day?):*
[They train full-time to race in short sprints (like the 100m or 200m) at major track meets around the world. Day-to-day, they practice on the track, lift weights, watch film to fix their running form, eat right, and focus on recovery like physical therapy. They also work with sponsors and manage their personal brand.]

**Typical Education Required:**
[High school diploma minimum. Most pro runners compete in college (NCAA Division I) while getting a 4-year degree (like Information Science) before signing a professional running contract.]

**Key Skills Required** *(list at least 6):*
1.Fast Acceleration and Top-Speed Running
2.Explosive Power and Strength
3.Self-Discipline and Strict Daily Habits
4.Mental Focus Under High Pressure
5.Understanding Recovery and Injury Prevention
6.Personal Branding and Talking to the Media

**Key Knowledge Areas Required:**
[Sprint biomechanics and running technique, sports nutrition, recovery methods, anti-doping rules (USADA/WADA), and brand marketing/sponsorships.]

**Median Annual Salary:**
[bout $66,000 to $83,000 a year. (Pay varies a lot: elite pro sprinters with top shoe deals earn $100,000 to $500,000+, while newer pros starting out make around $30,000 to $60,000).]

**Job Growth Outlook** *(growing, stable, declining?):*
[Stable, but very competitive. Openings depend on hitting fast time standards and qualifying for major meets rather than regular job growth.]


**Typical Career Path** *(how do people enter and advance in this field?):*
Standout high school runner → Compete in college (NCAA Division I) → Hit international qualifying times → Sign with a sports agent and shoe sponsor (like Nike or Adidas) → Compete in pro meets worldwide.

**3 specific courses or experiences that would build skills for this career:**
1.Competing on a Division I college track and field team
2.A college course in Kinesiology or Exercise Science (learning how the body moves)
3.A college course in Social Media & Personal Branding (learning how to handle sponsorships)
