# Four-Year Degree Plan Activity

A degree plan makes your academic direction tangible. It transforms your POV Statement, career research, and SMART goals from ideas into a structured roadmap. This draft is your starting point — it will evolve, and that is expected.

---

## Step 1 — Gather Your Materials

Before building your plan, collect the following:

- University of Tennessee, Knoxville (UTK)
- Applied Cybersecurity (B.S.) / CECS
- Fall 2026 First-Year Student
- Notes on your preferred major(s)

## Step 4 — Plan Your Electives Intentionally

Based on your Career-to-Degree Map, identify 4–6 elective courses you would like to take that support your career direction. These might include:

**My Intentional Electives:**

1.COSC 102: Introduction to Computer Science — Helps me learn basic Python coding so I can write simple scripts to automate security tasks and read log files.
2.AI 101: Introduction to the World of AI — Teaches me how basic AI tools work and how security teams use them to spot threats faster.
3.INSC 312: Fundamentals of Information Security — Shows me how companies protect their data and follow basic security rules and guidelines.
4.CYBR 465: Cybersecurity for Network-Controlled Systems — Teaches me how to protect real-world machines and equipment connected to the internet.
5.JREM 175: Principles of Digital Media — Builds my communication skills so I can write clear reports and explain tech stuff to everyday people.
6.CMST 210: Public Speaking — Gives me confidence to present security plans, lead team meetings, and talk to managers clearly.

## Step 6 — Reflect

Write a short reflection (8–12 sentences):

- What was most difficult about building this draft?
- What gaps or uncertainties did you notice?
- How does this plan reflect your POV Statement and SMART Goals?
- What do you most want to discuss with your academic advisor when you meet?

**My Reflection:**

Building this degree plan draft was harder than I thought because balancing my track schedule with all these cybersecurity classes takes a lot of planning. The main thing that gave me trouble was trying to map out all the prerequisites without messing up the flow of my semesters. I noticed a few gaps, like not knowing the exact semester every upper-level class is offered or how my minor credits fit in. Right now, I am focusing on keeping my grades up while competing at a high level on the track team. This plan matches my goals because it sets me up to get real tech skills and certifications as a backup, but keeps my primary focus on going pro in track. When I meet with my academic advisor, I want to double-check my class order so I don't get stuck taking classes that clash with my training or travel schedule. I also want to make sure I'm taking the right electives to keep my options open after graduation. Overall, having this written down makes it a lot easier to see how I can handle both sports and school over the next four years. It gives me a clear roadmap so I don't get stressed out when track season gets busy with travel. Having a solid plan for my major lets me put 100% into track knowing my degree is completely on track no matter what.
